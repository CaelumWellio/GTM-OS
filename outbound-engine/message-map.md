# What to say to schools, and the three things I would bet on

This is a view on what to say to a school and who to say it to, sitting on top of the evidence generated below, which is produced by the pipeline and can be regenerated at any time. No angle has been proven to win, so the confident parts of this page are one negative and a way of writing, not a ranked list of hooks. Three bets are set out at the end, each with the number that pays it off and the number that kills it written down before it runs. Two of them run this term. The third cannot be read at the size our list allows, so it waits for Term 1, and the arithmetic that decided that sits with it.

## What I would say to each of them

The block above prints four quotes a role, to keep it short enough to read. Eight a role are stored in `role_voice.json`, and where I quote something below that is not printed above, that is where it came from.

**The wellbeing team. 264 deals, and the people we mostly write to.**

What they raise, in their words: budget they do not hold ("the cost is beyond our budget expectations for the intended use in our context"), access they want to widen ("Would you be able to give me another sign in so that my other colleague could take a look at the lessons?"), and a trial they cannot fund ("We won't go through with the trial as we do not have it in the budget to continue seeing out the program"). The one price quote from a won deal reads differently: "I have arranged for our budget next year to include the cost of Wellio."

What to say back: when a wellbeing contact asks for another login, give it in the same reply, and in that reply ask two questions. Who signs off the spend, and when next year's budget is set. If you get both answers you have a deal. If you get neither after two asks you have an enthusiast without a budget line, which is most of this group.

What to stop saying: stop sending more product to someone who has just told you they need to show it to somebody else. Widen the access and narrow the question.

From the table: a reply from this role goes with 40.9 percent against 44.5 percent when they do not write back. I am not going to use that number, and the reason matters more than the number does. Below I take apart the leadership plus 6.7 as probably the deal escalating rather than us choosing. The minus 3.5 has the same weakness running the other way: a deal that is stuck stays with the wellbeing contact precisely because it never gets escalated, so this role keeps writing back on deals that were already going nowhere. One objection kills both numbers, and I am not going to apply it only to the one that suits me. So nothing above rests on the minus 3.5, and I withdraw the advice I used to hang on it, which was to stop reading their reply as progress. What to ask this role rests on the quotes instead, and they say plainly that this role does not hold the budget. That holds whichever way the number runs. Judgement: everything about what to ask them, drawn from eight quotes.

**The principal and their deputies. 211 deals.**

What they raise, in their words: decisions and dates ("we are actually meeting this week to decide on the programs we will take on. I'll be able to give you a definite answer by the end of this week"), the whole portfolio and its cost ("we're not in a position to take on any additional programs that come with a cost at this stage"), and capacity in years rather than terms ("It's something we may be able to consider down the track, perhaps in a few years, once we're better structured and resourced"). One of them wrote that they would be "requiring feedback on the benefit of subscribing or not, very shortly", and we lost it.

What to say back: write to leadership about a decision and a date, never about content. Name what the school would take on, what it sits beside or replaces, what it costs for the whole school for a year, and the date you need an answer by. If there is a trial, say in writing what the trial has to show for the answer to be yes, and who will judge it. A trial with no stated test answers nothing, and the quote above is a school telling us that in advance.

What to stop saying: stop the open-ended scheduling ask. Two fixed times, or a date you will call.

Now the trap. From the table, leadership engagement goes with 48.3 percent against 41.7 percent, and the obvious reading is that writing to the principal wins more deals. That is at least as likely to be backwards: a deal that is going well gets escalated, so leadership writing back is probably a symptom of a good deal rather than a cause of one. This project has already been caught by the same trap once, on won deals closing in 27 days against 73, which is mostly dead deals sitting open. Nothing in this table can separate the two, so nothing above depends on the causal reading being true. The same objection applies to the wellbeing minus 3.5 above, pointing the other way, and I have thrown that number out for the same reason rather than keeping whichever one flattered the advice I had already written. What the 6.7 points are good for is bet one, which is the only way to find out.

**Curriculum and teaching leads. 35 deals, the smallest cell that is not thin.**

The plus 14.4 is the largest difference in the table and the least trustworthy number on this page. Thirty-five deals can move twenty points on chance alone, and the method file puts the point where a difference of this size stops being noise at around 110 deals. I do not believe the number. I do believe the quotes, because they are unusually consistent and they are not about wellbeing at all.

What they raise, in their words: the timetable and the day falling apart. "we are still fighting for some time in the timetable". "is there anyway to meet 12.40. I have been given a last minute extra from a staff member who has just called in sick." "My DP has once again be called away. There was a major incident at lunch." And the one won deal in the set names the incumbent and the reason: "Year 7 and 8 will be continuing with TRP for next year, this decision was made by the Principal a while ago, based on this year's teacher evaluation and feedback."

What to say back: write to this role in timetable terms. Which year level, how many periods a term, who stands in front of the class, and what it sits beside in the line. That is a placement question, and it is the question they are actually asking.

What to stop saying: stop reading a reschedule as disinterest. Five of the eight things this role wrote are people moving a meeting while their day collapses, and they are the group that wins most often. Make rescheduling one click and keep going.

From the table: nothing I would defend. Judgement: all of it, from eight quotes.

**Business and IT. 5 deals.**

Not enough to go on. Five deals and one quote will not support a persona, and the win rate attached to them would move twenty points on two deals going the other way. I am not writing a paragraph about what this role cares about, because I would be making it up.

## What to stop saying

The line everyone quotes from the hook work is that talking about saving teachers time goes with losing: 32 percent against 45, thirteen points down on 108 deals, and wider in every cut, 26 points down in primary, 29 in the schools whose sector we never recorded, 18 in independent. Every cut points the same way, which is why it is the one negative I trust.

It does not say what people think it says, and I have checked. The counting rule in `customer-truth/method.md` is that a tag counts only when the school raised it, and our own mentions are stored without being counted. So those 108 deals are schools that told us they were stretched. They are not emails where we led with saving teachers time.

There are two readings. The words do it: putting the program next to workload invites a comparison a tired school has already lost once. Or the school does it: a school raising time pressure is telling us it has no capacity, and a school with no capacity buys less often whatever we say.

I believe the second, and not as a judgement call, because the counting rule puts the school's voice on every one of those 108 deals. The stored half points the same way, and it is now in the table above rather than in my head: on the 109 deals where only we raised time saving and the school never did, 46.8 percent were won. On the 108 where the school raised it, 32.4 percent. On the 625 where it never came up at all, 44.6 percent. Our own mentions cost nothing. The school's own mention costs twelve points against a deal where the subject never arose.

That table also shows this is not something peculiar to time saving. Four tags separate those two columns by eight points or more, and all four run the same way: when the school raises it the deal does worse, when only we raise it the deal does no worse and usually better. Whatever these tags are measuring, they are measuring the state the school is in when it writes to us, not the words we chose. It is still an association on closed deals like everything else here, so it is a reason to stop believing the copy rule, not a reason to believe its opposite.

So there are two things to stop, and only one of them is about copy.

Stop answering a school's time complaint with time relief. When a school says it is stretched, that is the clearest qualification signal in the whole file: those deals win 32 percent against the 43.4 percent average. Record it on the deal, and make the next email smaller rather than more reassuring: one year level, a fixed number of periods, a named teacher, a date. Do not agree that everyone is exhausted and then promise the program will fix it.

Stop quoting the thirteen points as a rule about our own wording, in either direction. We have never once recorded which angle an email led with, so on the question of what we should write, this file is silent. Bet two settles it.

## Three bets

None of these can be settled unless move three of the Term 4 plan lands first: the stamp on every send, three fields filled when a school is enrolled, which angle the email leads with, who it is aimed at, which wording version it is. Without it we are back where this page is, reading angles backwards out of text a school wrote and guessing who we aimed at. All three are settled on replies and meetings booked, not on wins, because at our volume one term cannot move a win rate far enough to read. The plan already puts volume behind the social media ban angle, so that is not a fourth bet here.

**The list these have to fit on.** Two months of AU outbound reaches about 1,020 schools. Between 307 and 396 of those are opening a fresh run after at least ninety days of silence, which is the only group where the question of who gets the first email means anything: three separate two-month windows gave 396, 400 and 307, so I plan on 300. Of that fresh-run list, about 93 percent have somebody in leadership on file, about 72 percent have a wellbeing contact, and about 14 percent have a curriculum or teaching lead. Those counts are mine, taken off the cached email and contact files rather than produced by the pipeline. They size the bets and nothing else on this page rests on them.

**Which runs first.** Bets one and three are the same bet wearing different clothes. Both change who gets the first email, both read on replies and meetings by 16 December, and both draw on that one list. Run together they get about 70 schools an arm and neither of them reads. So bet one runs in Term 4, bet three waits for Term 1, and bet two runs alongside bet one on the schools bet one is not using. The previous version of this page said it would run bet three first. That was wrong, and the 14 percent above is why. What bet three needs from bet one is the contact sourcing done on bet one's control arm, set out under bet three.

**How the gaps below were set.** Each bet names its arms, its metric, the gap that pays it off and the result that drops it, before it runs. The gaps are not gaps I would like. They are the smallest gaps a list that size can tell apart from chance, on the same arithmetic that puts the line at 110 deals elsewhere in this work. Anything narrower gets argued about in December by whoever likes the answer least, and that argument is the thing I am trying to make impossible.

**How the money below was worked out.** One chain, every link an assumption: a booked meeting qualifies about 66 percent of the time, 99 percent of qualified leads get a deal, 36 percent of those deals are won, and the average recorded deal in 2026 is $7,394. One extra booked meeting is therefore worth about $1,740 of won business. The rates underneath that chain come from files that only ever see schools which became leads, so they flatter us. Read every dollar below as a ceiling, not an estimate.

**Bet one. Address leadership early, on purpose. Runs from the first send of October.**

Arms: the schools on the AU fresh-run list for October and November that have a leadership contact already on file. That was 365 schools in one of the windows I counted and 288 in the other, so I plan on 280 and take the first 280 that qualify, assigned by alternating down the list as schools are enrolled so both arms run the same weeks and the same kinds of school. 140 treated, 140 control. On the treated 140 the first email goes to the leadership contact, stamped as such, written the way described above: the decision, what it replaces, the whole-school price, the date. The control 140 keeps current practice, untouched.

Metric: the share of schools where somebody in a leadership role writes back within fourteen days of the first send, and the share where a meeting is booked within thirty days. Both read on 16 December off the who-it-was-aimed-at stamp, the reply date on the timeline and the meeting-booked date on the lead record, three of which Monday's pull already reads.

Pays off: leadership replies at least 12 points more often on the treated 140 than on the control 140, and meetings are booked at least 10 points more often. Both, not either.

Drops: a leadership reply gap of 12 points or less. Drops too if the reply gap clears 12 and the meeting gap does not clear 10, which is a polite answer we bought and nothing else.

Worth: at the paying-off threshold, 10 points across 140 schools is 14 extra booked meetings, about $24,000 of won business from one arm in one term. If it holds and we then write to leadership first everywhere, the fresh-run list runs to roughly 1,800 schools a year and 10 points across the 93 percent of them that have a leadership contact is about 165 meetings, roughly $290,000 a year. That is the prize, and it is the most optimistic reading available, because a real effect is at least as likely to fall short of this bar as clear it, in which case the test says nothing and the prize is zero.

Costs: 140 schools worked differently from the way we work them today. If leadership-first is worse by the same 10 points, that is 14 meetings we do not book, about $24,000 of pipeline given up to find out. The rest is time rather than money: half a day of mine to write the leadership-first email and the brief, under an hour a week of SDR time picking the leadership contact on about 16 schools a week for nine weeks, and a day of mine in December to read it.

What it does not settle is whether this wins more deals. It settles whether leadership engagement is something we can cause at all, which is the part the table cannot tell us and the part the whole causal reading rests on.

**Bet two. Hold time saving still and see if it matters. Runs alongside bet one.**

Arms: the AU schools sent to in October and November that bet one is not using, about 740, split in half. On 370 the email leads with saving teachers time, stamped as that angle. On 370 it leads with placement, stamped as that. Bet one's 280 are excluded because their treated half is already committed to one angle, so they cannot also be split on angle without the two bets standing on each other.

Metric: reply rate within fourteen days and meeting booked within thirty, read on 16 December.

Pays off: the two halves land within 7 points of each other on reply rate and within 6 points on meeting rate. That is the bet. It is a bet on nothing happening, and it retires the copy rule.

Drops: either half beats the other by more than 6 points on meetings. Wider for placement means our wording does real damage, which is the reading I have argued against all through this page, and I am wrong. Wider for time saving means the phrase we have been treating as forbidden actually helps, which is a different way of being wrong and a worse one.

The second half of this bet cannot be read this term, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Flagging the deals where the school raises time pressure first only counts on deals that have closed. 108 of the last 842 carried it, and the line this work uses elsewhere puts a difference of this size out of reach below about 110 deals. Producing 110 closed deals carrying the flag took the last fourteen months. So the field goes in from October and gets used on the deal as a qualification flag from day one, I report no number on it in December, and the earliest honest read is late 2027. Saying that now is cheaper than discovering it in December.

Costs: no list is given up, because both halves are real sends that were going out anyway. What is risked is that one half is worse. At the smallest gap this test can read, 6 points across 370 schools, that is 22 meetings and about $39,000 of pipeline, and I would not learn it until the end. Time: half a day of mine for the two copy variants, and the Sales Manager holding one angle still across a list for six weeks, which is sign-off the plan already asks for.

**Bet three. Write to curriculum leads about the timetable. Does not run this term.**

A curriculum or teaching lead is on file at about 14 percent of the schools on a fresh-run list: 65 of 396 in one window, 36 of 307 in another. A treated arm of 50 or 60 schools cannot tell apart a reply gap narrower than about 18 points, and I do not believe writing to a curriculum lead first is worth 18 points. Running it in October would spend the list and produce a number I would have to disown in December, which is the exact thing I said on the page above that I would not do.

So it waits, and bet one is what it waits for. Bet one's control 140 is worked the current way for nine weeks regardless, and the job attached to it is to find and record a curriculum or teaching lead at every school on that list which has none. That is about 130 contacts to source, roughly five minutes each, call it two SDR days spread across the term. It is the only input bet three is missing.

Bet three then runs in Term 1 2027 on the February and March fresh-run list, once at least 180 schools on it carry a curriculum contact. Arms: 180 treated, where the first email goes to the curriculum or teaching lead and leads on placement, which year, how many periods, who delivers it, what it sits beside; 180 control on current practice. Same two metrics as bet one, read at the end of that term instead of this one.

Pays off: curriculum leads reply at least 10 points more often than the control and meetings are booked at least 9 points more often. Drops: either gap short of that. Worth at the threshold, 9 points across 180 schools, is 16 meetings, about $28,000 in the term it runs, on the same chain and the same ceiling warning as bet one.

If the sourcing has not reached 180 schools with a curriculum contact by the end of January, bet three does not run at all, and I will say so rather than run it small.

## What would change my mind

**Bet one.** The number that drops it is a leadership reply gap of 12 points or less between the treated 140 and the control 140. That would mean we cannot make leadership engage by aiming at them, the 48.3 percent in the table is the deal escalating rather than us choosing, and the answer is to go back to earning the escalation instead of trying to buy it. A reply gap over 12 with a meeting gap of 10 or less drops it as well: we bought a polite answer and nothing else. Either way I stop writing leadership-first and the $290,000 is worth nothing.

**Bet two.** The number that drops it is a meeting gap wider than 6 points between the two halves of 370, in either direction. Level, meaning inside 6 points on meetings and 7 on replies, is what I have bet on. I should say which way I expect to be wrong if I am: wider for placement, because that is the reading everyone already believes and I have spent this page arguing against it.

**Bet three.** Two things drop it before it starts: fewer than 180 schools carrying a curriculum contact by the end of January, or bet one showing that who we aim the first email at moves nothing at all, in which case there is no reason to test a second role. Once running, it drops on a reply gap of 10 points or less against the control, or a reply gap clearing 10 with a meeting gap of 9 or less. Then the plus 14.4 in the table was 35 deals being 35 deals, and I said in advance that I did not believe it.

**All three.** If the stamp is not live before the first send of October, all three stop before they start. I would rather say that than run them unstamped and hand you a number in December that I cannot defend.

**The money, not the bets.** Every dollar on this page runs through a 36 percent win rate and a $7,394 average deal, applied to meetings nobody has booked yet. If Term 4 closes at a materially different average deal, the order these bets sit in by value changes, and I would re-read them before spending a second term running them in the same order.
